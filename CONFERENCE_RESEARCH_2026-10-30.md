# ARES conference and paper research

Checked: 30 October 2026, Asia/Calcutta. Research conducted with three delegated agents and primary organizer/institution/society sources. No submission or registration has been made.

## Scope used to assess fit

One ESP32-S3 sensing node and one Raspberry Pi ground station; camera, presence radar, spot IR temperature sensor, GPS, LoRa telemetry, and proposed Wi-Fi images. The operator selects the disaster profile, which loads predefined sensor weights. Automatic disaster classification is excluded. Synthetic testing is the first milestone; real detection accuracy, aerial performance, and 3D reconstruction have not been demonstrated.

## Recommended paper direction

Working title: **ARES: Operator-Configured Multimodal Human-Presence Sensing with Event-Driven LoRa Telemetry**.

Primary question: do operator-selected profiles improve detection over a single global weight set under measured disturbances? Integration and manual weight selection alone do not establish novelty. The contribution must be supported by comparisons, repeatable experiments, and a justified method.

Baseline comparisons: individual sensors, equal-weight fusion, one global weight set, and selected-profile fusion. Include wrong-profile selections and unavailable/stale observations. Tune weights on development sessions; evaluate on separate sessions and people. Report precision, recall, false alerts per hour, latency, and results for each condition. Assess whether the spot thermometer adds useful evidence rather than assuming it does.

## Alternative research topics

1. **Event-driven LoRa reporting.** Compare periodic messages against event-triggered messages plus heartbeats. Measure actual application delivery rate, tail alert latency, missed transitions, airtime, bytes/event, and measured energy/event. Clearly labelled synthetic events can drive real radio experiments.
2. **Edge-ground partitioning.** Compare ESP-only inference, Pi-only inference, and a defined split. Measure accuracy, latency, peak RAM, bandwidth, and power. Image transfer remains on Wi-Fi; LoRa carries compact results.
3. **Failure-aware assessment.** Inject sensor timeouts, old data, radio outages, queue overload, and restarts. Compare detection of faults, recovery time, evidence age, and false confident decisions with a basic baseline.
4. **Uncertainty-aware route suggestions.** Use a calibrated mock scene and ground-truth obstacles. Compare unknown-aware planning with planning that treats unknown cells as free; report invalid routes, route length, availability, and update latency.
5. **Situation display.** Use the 3D terrain/dashboard as the demo interface. A standalone research claim requires an evaluation, such as interpretation accuracy and task completion time against a simpler display.

These are proposed research questions, not claims of novelty or completed results. Synthetic readings do not establish detection performance. One node cannot validate mesh networking or multi-node placement, and one stationary camera does not establish detailed 3D wreckage reconstruction.

## Recommended sequence

1. Establish a narrow sensing/fusion question and a repeatable experiment.
2. Prefer **ICEMCSI** if results can be ready by January; consider **ICATIECE** for a longer experimental runway.
3. Retain **CONECCT** as a provisional opportunity pending its complete call.
4. Consider **COMSNETS demo/poster** only if an honest, concrete submission can be ready by the November deadline.
5. Keep TRIDENT/BHTC on the watchlist; consider ICOSICS only if security becomes an actual research component.

Do not submit substantially the same paper simultaneously to multiple venues. Match any indexing requirement to the exact edition and track, rather than relying on an IEEE logo or template.
